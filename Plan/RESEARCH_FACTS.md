# Eco-Fact Research Strategy

**Version:** 1.0
**Created:** March 2026
**Status:** Planning

---

## Overview

This document outlines the methodology for researching and curating
365 daily eco-facts for the Seed app. Each fact must be scientifically
backed, include a source URL, and be localized in EN/ES/JA.

Facts are aligned to the UN International Days calendar where
possible, so that the daily fact resonates with what the world is
talking about on that day.

---

## Research Goals

1. **Curate 365 facts** covering sustainability, climate, nature,
   and the 17 UN SDGs
2. **Ensure accuracy** with verifiable scientific sources
3. **Include source URLs** for every fact (mandatory, no exceptions)
4. **Align to UN World Days** where a relevant day falls on that date
5. **Support localization** with EN/ES/JA translations
6. **Balance categories** across 5 fact types
7. **Start with 100** well-researched facts, expand to 365 over time

---

## Fact Categories

Each fact belongs to one of 5 categories. Target an even distribution
across the year (~73 per category for 365 total).

| Category | Target | Style | Example |
|----------|--------|-------|---------|
| Myth Busters | ~73 | "Actually, [common belief] is wrong because..." | "Rinsing recyclables is unnecessary. A quick scrape is enough." |
| Surprising Comparisons | ~73 | "Doing X saves the same CO2 as Y" | "Skipping one beef meal saves the same CO2 as not driving 15 km." |
| Positive News | ~73 | "Renewable energy now powers X% of..." | "The ozone layer is on track for full recovery by 2066." |
| Individual Impact | ~73 | "If everyone did X, it would save..." | "If every US household replaced one beef meal/week, it would equal removing 7.6M cars." |
| Nature Wonders | ~73 | "Did you know that [natural fact]..." | "A single mature tree absorbs 48 lbs of CO2/year." |

## Research Methodology

### Step 1: Assign UN World Day Facts First

For each of the ~75 UN/environmental days in the calendar above:
1. Research 1 compelling fact related to the day's theme
2. Ensure the fact connects to the relevant SDG(s)
3. Find a source URL from Tier 1-2 sources
4. Write the fact in a surprising, memorable style

### Step 2: Fill Remaining Days by Category

For the ~290 remaining days:
1. Distribute evenly across the 5 categories
2. Ensure no category has more than 3 consecutive days
3. Spread SDG coverage across the year (don't cluster all
   SDG 13 facts in one month)
4. Vary the tone: alternate between alarming stats, hopeful
   progress, and fascinating nature facts

### Step 3: Research Each Fact

For each fact:
1. Search Tier 1 sources first for the core claim
2. Verify the specific number/statistic is current (within 3 years)
3. Find the most authoritative source URL
4. Cross-reference with a second source if possible
5. Note if the data is US-specific, UK-specific, or global

### Step 4: Write the Fact

Each fact must be:
- **1-3 sentences** (concise enough to read in 10 seconds)
- **Surprising or counterintuitive** (reward for completing challenge)
- **Specific** (include a number: "30%", "22 kg", "7.6 million")
- **Sourced** (every fact has a URL, no exceptions)
- **Actionable where possible** (connect to something the user can do)

### Step 5: Localize

For each fact:
1. Translate to Spanish (ES) and Japanese (JA)
2. Adapt region-specific stats where needed (e.g., US-specific
   facts may need a global equivalent for JA/ES users)
3. Ensure source URLs work internationally

### Step 6: Validate

Before finalizing:
- [ ] Fact is accurate and current (data within 3 years)
- [ ] Source URL is live and leads to the claim
- [ ] Fact is 1-3 sentences
- [ ] Fact includes at least one specific number
- [ ] EN/ES/JA versions convey the same meaning
- [ ] Category is correctly assigned
- [ ] Related SDGs are identified
- [ ] UN World Day alignment is noted (if applicable)

## Quality Standards

### What Makes a Good Eco-Fact

**Strong facts:**
- "Aluminum can be recycled infinitely with no loss of quality.
  Recycling one can saves enough energy to power a TV for 3 hours."
  (Source: Aluminum Association)
- "The fashion industry produces 10% of global CO2 emissions -- more
  than international flights and maritime shipping combined."
  (Source: UNEP)

**Weak facts (avoid):**
- "Recycling is good for the environment." (too vague, no number)
- "Climate change is real." (not surprising, not actionable)
- "You should eat less meat." (preachy, not a fact)

### Source URL Requirements

Every fact must include a URL that:
- Links directly to the claim or its underlying data
- Is from a Tier 1-2 source (government, academic, major NGO)
- Is likely to remain live (prefer institutional URLs over blog posts)
- Is accessible without a paywall where possible

Acceptable URL patterns:
- `https://www.unep.org/...`
- `https://ourworldindata.org/...`
- `https://www.epa.gov/...`
- `https://www.nature.com/articles/...`
- `https://www.ipcc.ch/...`
- `https://drawdown.org/solutions/...`

Avoid:
- Social media links
- News articles that may be paywalled or deleted
- Personal blogs without institutional backing
- URLs with session tokens or tracking parameters

## Phased Approach

### Phase A: Initial 100 Facts (MVP)

Write 100 high-quality facts covering:
- All ~75 UN World Day-aligned facts
- 25 additional facts to fill gaps
- Even category distribution
- All 17 SDGs represented at least twice

With 100 facts, the app cycles every ~3.5 months. Users are
unlikely to notice repetition in the first few months.

### Phase B: Expand to 200 Facts

Add 100 more facts:
- Fill months with sparse UN day coverage (Aug, Dec)
- Deepen coverage of popular categories
- Add more "Positive News" to balance alarming stats

### Phase C: Complete 365 Facts

Fill remaining slots:
- One unique fact per day of the year
- Final balance pass across categories and SDGs
- Full localization review

## Common Pitfalls

1. **Outdated stats** -- renewable energy data changes fast. Always
   check the publication year. Prefer data from 2022 or later.
2. **Region-specific claims** -- "Americans produce 4.4 lbs of trash
   per day" is US-only. Either use global stats or note the region.
3. **Misleading comparisons** -- "equivalent to X cars off the road"
   sounds impressive but can be misleading at scale. Include the
   actual CO2 number alongside the comparison.
4. **Citation drift** -- URLs break. Prefer stable institutional
   URLs (un.org, epa.gov, ourworldindata.org) over news sites.
5. **Greenwashing traps** -- avoid facts that oversimplify (e.g.,
   "paper bags are better than plastic" -- they're not always).
   The RESEARCH_ACTIONS.md documents several counterintuitive
   findings; eco-facts should reflect the same intellectual honesty.
